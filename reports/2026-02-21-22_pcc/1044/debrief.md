## 1044 "Flash" — PCC 2026 (Feb 21–22, San Diego)
- **Official: 12-16-17-18-26, 18th of 42 overall (63 net).**
  - Saturday 14th on the day; Sunday 21st.
- Five windward-leewards on Coronado Roads, northwest breeze, 42 boats. Only 1044, Chomp and Mojo had data, so gaps below are among those three.
- **First of the three tracked boats in every race:** 7:11 ahead of Chomp and 9:48 ahead of Mojo over the regatta.
- The margin was built upwind: fastest of the three on 7 of 12 beats, on the shortest path, and usually on the side that paid.
- In this fleet a place was worth about 10 s, so a clean leeward exit or first run is worth a few places a race.

**What went well**
- **Upwind: the best angle and the shortest path**
  - Over the ground (GPS track), 1044 tacks through about 81° on average, against 88–89° for Chomp and Mojo, at the same boat speed.
  - Sailed about 34% more than the straight line on the beats, against 41–42% for the others.
  - Carries the most heel of the three (18.3° on average upwind) and changes gears. On Saturday's slower beats it opened its angle to 89–93° and was 6–11% faster than Mojo.
- **Side selection: usually on the paying side**
  - Left on race 1's beats, right on race 2 beat 2 (87% right, 42–50 s faster than the other two), left on race 3 beat 2 with the shift.
  - Race 3 beat 1: stayed near the middle while Chomp went to the right corner and lost 2:24.
- **Sunday starts: clean**
  - Race 4: 1 s late at 5.5 kt; race 5: on the line. After Saturday's two costly starts, that's the fix.

**Top 3 to work on**
1. **Leeward mark exits**
   - Saturday: out of the leeward mark at 3.1–3.5 kt every time, against 4.2–4.9 kt for Chomp, and 73–108 s back to speed against 27–64 s. The most lost at leeward marks of the three (89 m on average).
   - Sunday was better: 4.9 kt in two of three, but 2.3 kt out of race 5's first leeward gate.
   - **Next time:** drop earlier, go wide in and tight out, trim main and jib on through the turn, and foot to full speed before pointing.
2. **First runs: Chomp gained twice on Saturday**
   - Race 2: 10:47 against Chomp's 10:18 (+29 s); race 3: 9:29 against 9:02 (+27 s). Chomp gybed once on each; 1044 didn't gybe.
   - **Next time:** someone looks aft for pressure all the way down, and gybe to stay in the puffs and on the headed gybe rather than holding one gybe to the layline.
3. **Sunday's first beats**
   - Race 5 beat 1: 48 s behind Chomp at the first mark, the biggest first-beat deficit of the regatta (race 2's was 4 s). It got the lead back on the second run.
   - Sunday overall: fastest on 2 of 5 beats (5 of 7 on Saturday), and 81 s lost upwind against 14 s on Saturday.
   - **Next time:** on a new day, take a couple of reference tacks before the start to re-check the angles and the pressure pattern rather than carrying Saturday's plan over.

**Data notes**
- Official places are from YachtScoring (event 50508, 42 boats). Its finish times aren't used (some are data-entry artifacts); gaps come from the GPS tracks.
- Speeds are over ground. The logged wind is a smoothed weather-model feed, so there are no speed targets; the other two boats are the benchmark.
- Tacking angle over the ground is from GPS course and doesn't depend on the compass. 1044's heading reads only 1–3° from its track upwind, which is low for a keelboat, so heading-based angles are shown but not leaned on.
- Only three boats had data. "Fastest", "order" and "which side paid" are among these three, not the whole fleet.
