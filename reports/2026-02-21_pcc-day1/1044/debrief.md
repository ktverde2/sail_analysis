## 1044 — PCC 2026, Day 1 (Feb 21, San Diego)
- Three windward-leewards with an offset at the top and a gate at the bottom, northwest breeze (about 285–316°).
- First of the three tracked boats in every race: ahead of Chomp by 2:28, 0:42 and 2:13, and of Mojo by 3:17, 1:35 and 3:40. (Only these three had data; the rest of the fleet isn't in Njord.)
- The gap came upwind: fastest of the three on 5 of 7 beats, on the shortest path, and usually on the side that paid.
- The time 1044 gave away was at the starts, on the first runs, and out of the leeward marks. That's where the next minute is.

**What went well**
- **Upwind: the best angle and the shortest path**
  - Over the ground (GPS track), 1044 tacks through about 81° on average, against 88–89° for Mojo and Chomp, at the same boat speed.
  - Sailed about 35% more than the straight line on the beats, against 43% for the others. On race 1's first beat that was 1.25 nm, against 1.35 for Mojo and 1.42 for Chomp.
  - Carries the most heel of the three (18° on average upwind) and changes gears. On race 1 beat 2 and race 3's last beat, it opened its tacking angle by compass to 89–93° and was 6–11% faster than Mojo.
- **Side selection: usually on the side that paid**
  - Left on race 1's beats (21–30% right), and right on race 2 beat 2 (87% right, 42–50 s faster than the other two).
  - Left on race 3 beat 2 (11% right) when its own headings show the wind going left 10°.
  - Race 3 beat 1: stayed near the middle with 7 tacks while Chomp went to the right corner and lost 2:24.
- **Recovered from two poor starts to be first of the three at the first mark**
  - Race 3: over and restarted, 3.1 kt at the gun, and still first at the windward mark by 44 s.

**Top 3 to work on**
1. **Leeward mark exits: the slowest of the three**
   - Out of the leeward mark at 3.1–3.5 kt every time, against 4.2–4.9 kt for Chomp in three of four. Back up to speed in 73–108 s, against 27–64 s for Chomp.
   - 89 m lost per leeward rounding on average, the most of the three (Chomp 70 m, Mojo 85 m); 107 m in race 2.
   - Against Chomp at the same four gates that's about 75 m, roughly 25 s over the day, and it let Chomp stay close in race 2.
   - **Next time:** drop earlier, go wide in and tight out, trim main and jib on through the turn, and foot to full speed before pointing. The first 30 s after the mark sets up the next 90.
2. **Starts: two of three gave time away**
   - Race 2: 115 m back at −60 s (the furthest back of the three all day) and 10 s late at the gun, 72% up the line toward the boat end. Chomp was on the line and 4 s ahead at the first mark.
   - Race 3: 2.5 m over at the gun; back and restarted at +10 s, at 3.1 kt. Mojo was 2 s late at 5.6 kt.
   - Race 1: 4 s late at the pin end but slow, 4.6 kt at the gun (Mojo 5.6, Chomp 5.4), then went left and it paid.
   - **Next time:** be within 2–4 lengths of the line at 2:00 on early line sights, and build speed from −15 s. Near the line late, slow by heading up, not by bearing away.
3. **First runs: Chomp gained on 1044 twice**
   - Race 2: 10:47 against Chomp's 10:18 (+29 s); Chomp's margin grew from 4 s to 33 s. Race 3: 9:29 against 9:02 (+27 s).
   - Chomp gybed once on each first run; 1044 didn't gybe on them. Chomp's runs were 6.4 kt against 6.1–6.3 for 1044.
   - **Next time:** someone looks aft for pressure all the way down the run, and gybe to stay in the puffs and on the headed gybe rather than holding one gybe to the layline.

*Also worth a look:*

- **Windward roundings**

**Data notes**
- Speeds are over ground.
- The logged wind is a smoothed weather-model feed (identical on 1044 and Mojo, and it changes only every few minutes), so there are no speed targets. The other two boats are the benchmark.
- Only three boats had data. "Fastest", "order" and "which side paid" are among these three, not the whole fleet.
- Tacking angle over the ground is from GPS course and doesn't depend on the compass. 1044's heading reads only 1–3° from its track upwind, which is low for a keelboat, so heading-based angles are shown but not leaned on.
- Mark passages and finish times come from the course marks, the same way for every boat.
