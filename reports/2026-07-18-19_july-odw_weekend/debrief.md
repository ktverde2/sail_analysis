## July ODW, July 18–19, 2026 (San Diego)
- Five windward-leewards in a westerly: 263–268° Saturday, 275–283° Sunday.
- The boat is fast and the laylines are good.
- Most of the loss is at the marks: 563 m in 12 roundings, more than all our tacks and gybes cost.
- Next come the shifts (we tacked on lifts and went right by habit), then the last minute before the gun.

**What went well**
- **Upwind speed on the card (100–101% on Saturday)**
  - Saturday races 1 and 2 were at 100–101% of the 9 kt card; race 3 was light and short (96%).
  - Port and starboard within 0.15 kt of each other on 7 of 9 beats, so current isn't flattering one tack.
  - Sunday's 103% probably overstates it (see the data notes), but the boat was on the pace of a windier card.
- **Laylines fetched cleanly (5 of 8 windward marks)**
  - Almost never sat on a header: only one stretch all weekend of 45 s or longer sailing 5° headed (48 s, Saturday race 2).
- **Sunday race 1 start: on the line, at speed**
  - On the line at the gun, mid-line, accelerating through it: 5.0 kt at the gun, 5.8 kt at +10 s.
  - That's the template.

**Top 3 to work on**
1. **Roundings: 563 m lost, mostly on the exit**
   - Windward roundings cost 44 m on average, leeward 53 m. The goal is zero.
   - Next milestone: match our best every time (24 m windward, 22 m leeward). That alone saves 283 m.
   - In 9 of 12 roundings we lost more in the minute after the mark than in the 30 s before it.
   - *Windward:* the two worst exits reached off at 130–150° for 15–40 s before bearing away. The rest ran at 159–170°. Those two were Saturday race 1's first mark (53 m) and Saturday race 3's (87 m).
   - *Windward:* the first mark of a race cost more than the second every time (53 vs 31, 44 vs 24, 51 vs 32 m), which looks like a routine still being found.
   - *Leeward:* we came out at 4.3–4.8 kt, against 5.7–6.0 kt upwind.
   - *Leeward:* Sunday race 1 pinched out (26° to the wind by heading, down to 4.0 kt). Sunday race 2 went wide and slow (43°, down to 3.4 kt, 80 s to settle, 84 m, the weekend's worst).
   - The Etchells carries its momentum into the mark, so an early drop costs little; a clean exit with everyone hiking is worth far more.
   - **Next time:** windward, prep in the last 100 m (lever eased, traveler down, kite sheet on its mark), bear away to the run angle, *then* hoist. Leeward, drop a few lengths early, go wide in and tight out, and foot to 5.5 kt before pointing.
   - **Drill:** six roundings of each in a row, timed from three lengths out until back on target.
2. **Shifts: tacked on lifts and went right by habit**
   - 29 tacks away from the start and marks: 11 on a lift, only 5 on a header, 13 with no clear shift.
   - Eight of the 11 lift tacks left a lifted starboard tack for port.
   - Worked the right on 8 of 9 beats (70–100% of the time right of the rhumb line).
   - That included all three beats where the wind trended 5–9° left. In a trend, the boat nearest the new wind gains.
   - Trend clues to check before each race: headings on each tack drifting one way, the line getting steadily more pin-favored, one side that keeps gaining.
   - **Next time:** log the high, low and median heading on each tack before the start, and have the trimmer call the heading against the median every 30 s. Oscillating: tack when 5° below the median. Trending: sail toward the new wind and into the header.
3. **Starts: too far back at one minute**
   - We were 74–79 m (about 8 lengths) behind the line at −60 s in four of five races, then made up the gap with a long reach-in.
   - It worked once, when the timing was perfect (Sunday race 1, 5.8 kt at −30 s). The other three were 4, 6 and 13 s late.
   - Sunday race 2 was down to 2.1 kt at −30 s with 42 m still to go, and 21 m back at the gun.
   - The one close-in setup (36 m at −60 s, Saturday race 3) was on time but slowed through the gun (5.1 → 4.3 kt at +10 s). That usually means pointing too early, or bad air the data can't see. Foot for speed for the first minute.
   - **Next time:** from 2:00, hold within 2–4 lengths of the line on early line sights (5, 3 and 2 lengths back), with no big bear-aways in the last 45 s. Bow calls distance, one person calls time, and we build speed from about −15 s (40 m at 5 kt is 16 s).
   - **Drill:** timed runs to a buoy from a slow luff until the crew knows the day's acceleration time.

*Also worth a look:*
- **Laylines joined early**
  - Final tack 16–72 lengths from the windward mark on all eight beats (median about 35); the common guideline is inside ~10.
  - Five of eight still fetched cleanly, so it isn't costing metres in our data.
  - It costs places in traffic (bad air on the layline parade, getting tacked on) and in shifts (the three overstands were 7–11°).
  - Cross into a clear lane a few lengths short and tack onto the layline late.
- **Tacks**
  - 26–37% speed loss per tack, 256 m across the weekend (Saturday race 3 worst, 13 m per tack).
  - Slow in, faster through head-to-wind, come out a few degrees low and squeeze up.
  - Sunday race 2: 14 tacks against 7 in race 1, and six had no shift behind them. Were those for clear air?
- **Heel on Sunday**
  - 4–6° over the 9 kt card (22° and 20° against 16°), but that matches the card at about 13–15 kt, so it's more likely a wind-speed question (see the data notes).
  - If it really was 9–10 kt, test depowering (backstay, traveler down, jib at the outer spreader band) against the current setup, 2 minutes each.

**Data notes**
- Speeds are over ground (no paddlewheel).
- Wind speed is your 8–10 kt; Njord's logged wind is filler on both days.
- Sunday probably had more wind: 20–22° of heel and 5.87–5.88 kt match the card at about 13–15 kt (target 5.9–6.0 kt, heel 21–22°). If so, Sunday's "103% of target" was about on target, and the heel was right for the breeze.
- Wind direction and shifts come from our tacking headings. Single 3–4° calls are soft; weekend-long patterns are solid.
- Saturday's courses had no marks in Njord, so legs were detected from heel.
- No other boats were tracked: no fleet ranks, and no view of traffic at the marks, bad air on the laylines, or the spinnaker set and drop.
- Layline distances are straight-line from the last tack to the mark, in 9.3 m boat lengths.
- Start lateness is measured at 1 Hz; Njord's interpolated figures are within 1 s (3.1, 5.2, 0.7, −0.1, 12.9 s).

**Two questions**
- How much wind was there on Sunday, really? If it was 12–15 kt, Sunday's speed and heel were on the card, not over it.
- What happens on board in the first 30 seconds after the windward mark: who calls the bear-away, and when does the kite go up? The two slowest exits both reached off at 130–150° first, and that sequence is where the metres are.
