## July ODW, July 18–19, 2026 (San Diego)
Five windward-leewards in a westerly (263–268° Saturday, 275–283° Sunday). The boat is fast and the laylines are good. Most of the loss is at the marks: 563 m in 12 roundings, more than all our tacks and gybes cost. Next come the shifts: we tacked on lifts and went right by habit. Then the last minute before the gun.

**What went well**
- **Speed.** Upwind averaged 100–101% of the 9 kt card on Saturday, apart from the light, short race 3 (96%). Port and starboard were within 0.15 kt of each other on 7 of 9 beats, so current isn't flattering one tack. Sunday's 103% probably overstates it (see the data notes), but the boat was at least on the pace of a windier card.
- **Laylines.** Five of eight windward marks fetched cleanly. We almost never sat on a header: only one stretch all weekend of 45 s or more sailing 5° headed (48 s, Saturday race 2).
- **Sunday race 1 start.** On the line at the gun, mid-line, and accelerating through it (5.0 kt at the gun, 5.8 kt at +10 s). That's the template.

**Top 3 to work on**
1. **Roundings: set the boat up before the mark, not after it.** Windward roundings cost 44 m on average, leeward 53 m. The goal is zero. The next milestone is matching our best every time (24 m windward, 22 m leeward), which alone would have saved 283 m. In 9 of 12 roundings we lost more in the minute after the mark than in the 30 s before it, so the problem is the exit. **Windward:** the two worst exits reached off at 130–150° for 15–40 s before bearing away to the run angle. The rest ran at 159–170°. Those two were Saturday race 1's first mark (53 m) and Saturday race 3's (87 m). The first windward mark of a race cost more than the second every time (53 vs 31, 44 vs 24, 51 vs 32 m), which looks like a routine still being found. A good set is prepared on the last 100 m of the beat: lever eased, traveler down, kite sheet pre-set to its mark. Then the helm bears away to the run angle *at* the mark, so the trimmer never sheets hard on a reach. **Leeward:** we came out at 4.3–4.8 kt, against 5.7–6.0 kt upwind. Sunday race 1 pinched out (26° to the wind by heading, down to 4.0 kt). Sunday race 2 went wide and slow (43°, down to 3.4 kt) and took 80 s to settle, the worst rounding of the weekend at 84 m. The Etchells carries its momentum into the mark, so an early drop costs little, and a clean exit with everyone hiking is worth far more. **Next time:** at the windward mark, "prep at 100 m, bear away to the run angle, *then* hoist." At the leeward mark, drop a few lengths earlier than feels right, go wide in and tight out, and foot until the speed reads 5.5 kt before pointing. Drill: six roundings of each in a row, timed from three lengths out until back on target.
2. **Play the shifts, not the side.** Of 29 tacks away from the start and marks, 11 were on a lift and only 5 on a header (13 had no clear shift). Eight of the 11 left a lifted starboard tack for port. We worked the right on 8 of 9 beats, 70–100% of the time right of the rhumb line. That included all three beats where the wind trended 5–9° left, and in a trend, the boat nearest the new wind gains. Two habits would fix most of this. First, decide before each race whether the wind is oscillating or trending: a heading on each tack that drifts one way, a line that keeps getting more pin-favored, or one side that keeps gaining all say "trend". Second, measure every tack against the beat's median heading. **Next time:** in the prestart, sail long on each tack and write down the high, low and median headings. On the beat, the trimmer calls the heading against the median every 30 s. Oscillating: tack when 5° below the median, after sailing a few seconds into the header so it isn't just a lull. Trending: sail toward the new wind and into the header, and accept a couple of sterns to get there.
3. **Starts: be near the line at one minute.** We were 74–79 m (about 8 lengths) behind the line at −60 s in four of five races and made up the gap with a long reach-in. It worked when the timing was perfect (Sunday race 1, 5.8 kt at −30 s). It cost us when it wasn't: 4, 6 and 13 s late in the other three. Sunday race 2 was down to 2.1 kt at −30 s with 42 m still to go, and was 21 m back at the gun. The one race we started close in (36 m at −60 s, Saturday race 3) we were on time, but we slowed through the gun (5.1 → 4.3 kt at +10 s). Slowing right after the gun usually means pointing too early (or bad air, which the data can't see); for the first minute, foot for speed. **Next time:** from 2:00, hold position within 2–4 lengths of the line using line sights, taken early at 5, 3 and 2 lengths back. Make no big bear-aways on starboard in the last 45 s. The bow calls distance, one person calls time, and we build speed from about −15 s (40 m at 5 kt is 16 s). Drill: timed runs to a buoy from a slow luff until the crew knows the acceleration time for the day.

*Also worth a look:*
- **Laylines are joined early.** Our final tack came 16–72 lengths from the windward mark on all eight beats (median about 35). The common guideline is to join inside ~10 lengths. Five of the eight still fetched cleanly, so this isn't costing metres in our data. It will cost places in traffic (bad air on the layline parade, getting tacked on) and in shifts (the three overstands were 7–11°). Aim to cross into a clear lane a few lengths short and tack onto the layline late.
- **Tacks** lose 26–37% of speed and 256 m across the weekend (Saturday race 3 worst, 13 m per tack). Slow in, faster through head-to-wind, come out a few degrees low and squeeze up. Sunday race 2 had 14 tacks against 7 in race 1, and six of them had no shift behind them: were those for clear air?
- **Heel** reads 4–6° over the 9 kt card on Sunday (22° and 20° against 16°). That matches the card at about 13–15 kt, so it's more likely a wind-speed question than a heel problem (see the data notes). If it really was 9–10 kt, test depowering (backstay, traveler down, jib at the outer spreader band) against the current setup for 2 minutes each.

**Race notes**
- **Sat 1:** The best-played race. Both tacks on a real shift were on headers, with no lift tacks, and it had the cleanest leeward rounding of the weekend (22 m). The first windward mark gave back 53 m on a reaching exit.
- **Sat 2:** Three tacks on lifts. The second beat went 9° left while we were 94% on the right. We also overstood the first windward mark by ~10°.
- **Sat 3:** Near-perfect timing (1 s) from the only close-in setup, but slow through the gun (−0.7 kt) and the slowest race upwind (96% at 9 kt). Three of the four tacks were on lifts of 5–8°, with the deepest tacks of the weekend (37% speed loss). The windward mark cost 87 m, the most of any windward rounding.
- **Sun 1:** A great start, then the first beat's wind backed 7° while we worked the right. We overstood the second windward mark by ~11°.
- **Sun 2:** The late start (13 s) and the leeward gate (84 m) are where the time went. Fourteen tacks against seven in race 1; six of them had no clear shift behind them.

*Data notes: speeds are over ground (no paddlewheel). Wind speed is your 8–10 kt; Njord's logged wind is filler on both days. Sunday's numbers suggest more wind than that: 20–22° of heel and 5.87–5.88 kt match the card at about 13–15 kt (target 5.9–6.0 kt, heel 21–22°). If so, Sunday's "103% of target" was really about on target, and the heel was right for the breeze. Wind direction and shifts come from our tacking headings. Single calls of 3–4° are soft; weekend-long patterns are solid. Saturday's courses had no marks in Njord, so legs were detected from heel. No other boats were tracked, so there are no fleet ranks, and the data can't see traffic at the marks, bad air on the laylines, or the spinnaker set and drop. Layline distances are straight-line from the last tack to the mark, in 9.3 m boat lengths. Start lateness is measured at 1 Hz; Njord's interpolated figures are within 1 s (3.1, 5.2, 0.7, −0.1, 12.9 s).*

**Two questions:** How much wind was there on Sunday, really? If it was 12–15 kt, Sunday's speed and heel were on the card, not over it. And what happens on board in the first 30 seconds after the windward mark: who calls the bear-away, and when does the kite go up? The two slowest exits both reached off at 130–150° first, and that sequence is where the metres are.
