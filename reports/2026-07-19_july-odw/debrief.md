## July ODW, Sunday July 19, 2026 (San Diego)
Two windward-leewards in an 8–10 kt westerly around 275–285°, a little lighter in race 2. One very good start, one we'll fix, upwind speed on target, a lot more heel than the card asks for, and a habit of heading right even when the wind was going left.

**What went well**
- **Race 1 start was right on time.** On the line at the gun (within 1 m), middle of the line, and accelerating through it: 5.0 kt at the gun, 5.8 kt at +10 s.
- **Upwind speed on target.** Steady-state upwind averaged 5.87 kt in race 1 and 5.88 kt in race 2: 101–107% of the card across 8–10 kt, and the same on both tacks in three of the four beats.
- **Tacks recover quickly.** Back to 95% of entry speed in 13–14 s on average, costing about 4–6 m each against the wind. That's a solid baseline.

**Top 3 to work on**
1. **Race 2 start: parked too early.** At −60 s we were 77 m back. Speed then fell to 2.1 kt by −30 s with 42 m still to go. Even at 5 kt, 42 m takes about 16 s, so the go point was around −16 s. At −10 s we were still at 2.7 kt and 30 m back, crossed 13 s late at the boat end, and gave away the first few boat lengths of the race. **Next time:** from −30 s, call distance and time out loud and pull the trigger off the starting-ratio card, not by feel.
2. **Heel is well over the card.** The 9 kt target is 16°. We averaged 22° in race 1 and 20° in race 2, and in race 1 we spent about 60% of the upwind time more than 5° over. Speed didn't suffer, and leeway (heading vs. course over ground) stayed at 5.5–6° whatever the heel. So this isn't obviously costing us, but it doesn't match the card. Our speed and heel sit exactly where the card puts 13 kt (5.9 kt, 21°). Either the breeze was stronger than 8–10 kt, or we carry more heel than the card at this wind speed. **Next time:** in the first beat of the day, run 2 minutes at 16° heel, then 2 minutes at 22°, and compare the speed on the Vakaros. That tells us which one is right.
3. **We went right while the wind went left.** On three of the four beats we spent 77–80% of the time right of the rhumb line. On two of them (race 1 first beat, race 2 second beat) the wind backed 5–7° over the beat, which pays the left. We also tacked on lifts more than on headers: of 16 tacks away from the start and marks, 5 were on a lift, 3 on a header, and 8 had no clear shift. **Next time:** before each beat, call whether the wind is oscillating or trending, and pick the side from that. On starboard, stay while the compass shows a lift.

*Also worth a look:* tacks got deeper through the day (speed loss 24% → 32% per tack), and one layline overstood by ~11° (race 1, second beat).

**Race notes**
- **Race 1:** On the line at the gun, then even speed on both tacks. The first beat had one oddity: port tack was 0.35 kt faster than starboard (5.94 vs 5.59) with similar heel. That usually means current or a persistent shift, not trim.
- **Race 2:** Lighter: tacking angle opened from 67° to 72° and heel dropped from 22° to 20°, while speed stayed at 5.9 kt. The late start is where most of the time went. We also tacked 14 times against 7 in race 1; at about 6 m each, the extra tacks cost around 40 m. Whether that was shifts or traffic, the data can't say.
