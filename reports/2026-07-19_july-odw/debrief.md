## July ODW, Sunday July 19, 2026 (San Diego)
Two windward-leewards in a westerly around 275–285°, a little lighter in race 2. One very good start, one we'll fix, and upwind speed that held steady all day.

**What went well**
- **Race 1 start was right on time.** On the line at the gun (within 1 m), middle of the line, and accelerating through it: 5.0 kt at the gun, 5.8 kt at +10 s.
- **Upwind speed held up.** Steady-state upwind averaged 5.87 kt in race 1 and 5.88 kt in race 2, with the same numbers on both tacks in three of the four beats. The boat is going the same speed whoever is steering the numbers.
- **Tacks recover quickly.** Back to 95% of entry speed in 13–14 s on average, costing about 4–6 m each against the wind. That's a solid baseline.

**Top 3 to work on**
1. **Race 2 start: parked too early.** At −60 s we were 77 m back. Speed then fell to 2.1 kt by −30 s with 42 m still to go. Even at 5 kt, 42 m takes about 16 s, so the go point was around −16 s. At −10 s we were still at 2.7 kt and 30 m back, crossed 13 s late at the boat end, and gave away the first few boat lengths of the race. **Next time:** from −30 s, call distance and time out loud and pull the trigger off the starting-ratio card, not by feel.
2. **Tacks got deeper as the day went on.** Speed loss per tack was about 24% on the first beat of race 1 and 32% on the last beat of race 2, bottoming out near 4.0 kt instead of 4.3–4.4. Recovery time held, so this is the turn itself: more rudder or the jib coming in late. **Next time:** slower helm through the tack, then trim the jib on as the bow settles. Finish practice with sets of 10 tacks while tired.
3. **Race 2 had twice the tacks.** 14 tacks against 7 in race 1, on similar-length beats. At about 6 m each, the extra tacks cost around 40 m, or 15–20 s. Some of that may be tactics (shifts, traffic), which the data can't see. **Next time:** before each beat, decide what the shift pattern is and set a tack budget; tack on headers, not on nerves.
