# Etchells technique

Upwind speed, balance, gears and setup, from the sailmakers' guides and class experts. Speed and heel targets live in `../etchells-targets.md`; this file explains what to change when the numbers are off. Rig numbers differ between sailmakers and boats, so treat them as a starting point and log Mojo's own.

## Balance and heel
- The Etchells is sailed as one sail package, not main and jib separately. Get the helm balanced and the boat responds. [E1]
- The jib does most of the driving (about two-thirds by one estimate), so it deserves most of the trim attention. [E1, E2]
- Aim for a little weather helm. Too much and the boat wants to round up and won't track; too little and it feels mushy and falls off. Extra heel adds weather helm. [E3, E4]
- Hike first, then depower. Sustained hard hiking pays in this boat. [E2, E3]
- When the boat goes flat in a lull: backstay off, mainsheet out a touch, jib eased, jib tack/luff eased, weight in. Reverse as the pressure returns. [E4]
- **Data:** heel vs. card at the day's wind, and heel standard deviation. Heel well over card with speed on target → overpowered but coping: try depowering (backstay, traveler down, jib to the outer mark) and compare speed. Big heel spread → power cycles; the main trimmer should play the sheet and traveler more.

## Modes (high vs. fast)
- The boat is always either looking for power, in the power band, or overpowered. Decide which before changing anything. [E5]
- High mode (to hold a lane): a touch more jib sheet and traveler up or mainsheet on; in bumpy water traveler up rather than sheet on, keeping some twist to re-accelerate after waves. Accept being slightly slower. [E5]
- Fast/low mode (to get to a side): if overpowered, a little backstay and traveler down; if the power is right, just a little traveler down; if underpowered, bear away slightly and don't over-sheet the jib. Aim for only ~1–2% more speed (about 0.1 kt); overdoing it upsets heel and balance. [E5]
- Backstay and mainsheet go together: whenever you change the backstay, reconsider the mainsheet. [E5]
- **Data:** TWA vs. card with speed %. Wider than card with speed at or above target is a fast mode; wider and slow is a problem; narrower and slow is pinching.

## Gears (what each control does)
- **Backstay:** flattens both sails and firms the headstay (flatter jib). Ease it for power in light air (more headstay sag, fuller main, tighter main leech). [E5, E6]
- **Mast lever/ram:** stiffens (aft) or softens (forward) the bottom two-thirds of the mast. Build breeze → progressively lever aft for a firmer headstay. Keep it cleated; a floating mast pumps and changes sail shape over every wave. [E7, E6]
- **Traveler:** changes heel and helm; too high adds drag. [E5]
- **Jib sheet/leech:** trim to spreader marks so the jib leech sits between two bands; tighter as breeze builds, out at the outer mark in big breeze. Leech telltale on the edge of stalling. [E2, E6]
- **Jib luff (tack/halyard):** light scallops for power in light to medium air, hard in breeze to hold the draft forward. [E2, E6]
- **Inhaul:** pulls the jib inboard to point higher; ease it if the main lower luff is backwinding when overpowered. It's possible to over-inhaul: signs are an unsettled lower main luff and a boat that won't accelerate. [E5, E8]
- **Cunningham:** only from about 15 kt. [E2]
- **Headstay sag** is the hardest thing to see and repeat, and the biggest difference around the fleet. Judge it by the jib entry: too fine → needs more sag; too blunt → less. If the headstay is bouncing in waves, firm it up (lever aft, backstay, mainsheet). [E7]
- Smooth water likes flatter sails with harder leeches; rough water likes fuller sails with more twist. Venues with chop need a little less rake and slightly fuller shapes than flat-water tuning. [E9]

## San Diego: power for chop (2026 Worlds)
- In the bumpy, underpowered conditions of the 2026 Worlds, the leaders set up for power: traveller high (up to about 300 mm above centre), more mainsail twist, a deeper jib foot and the jib car a touch outboard. The traveller-down, tight-main setup used to keep the forestay firm, fast in flatter water, was less effective there. [W26]
- The point is to match the setup to the venue, and to study whatever the fastest boat nearby is doing. [W26]
- **Data:** in chop, heel below 19–20° with the boat beside you footing away and gaining toward the mark (side-by-side stretches, VMC) points to too little power. The test is VMC in the side-by-side stretches, not SOG.

## Setup references (starting points; check your sailmaker's guide)
- **North base (6–12 kt):** forestay about 48" from the gooseneck band mark to deck (longer by up to ¼" in light air, shorter in breeze); mast step 12'6½" aft of a mark 12' behind the partners; uppers set with the headstay at 10 on the Loos PT-2; lowers set to 10 then four turns off. Adjust uppers over ~5 turns and lowers over ~11 turns across the wind range (lowers more than uppers). [E2, E1]
- **Doyle base (9–11 kt):** forestay 48¼", uppers 19 and lowers 8 on the PT-2; +1 upper/+2 lower turns at 12–13 kt, −1/−2 below 10 kt, lightest −2/−3; mast step, forestay and lever get progressively firmer up the range; jib sheeted at 8° by default (7–7.5° max power, 9° in heavy air or very light air). [E6]
- **Backstay marks (Doyle):** mark the backstay where the headstay just goes firm (max-power reference); max depower mark ~140 mm above; lightest mark ~60 mm below. Makes settings repeatable. [E6]
- **Mast gap at the partners:** no gap (or slightly bent against the aft edge) in light air, about ¾" (20 mm) in heavy air. [E7]
- Return to base after each day and log turnbuckle counts, so fast settings aren't lost. [E2]
- The 2026 Worlds rig programme was simpler: most teams used the Spinlock rig gauge (base around 220, about 19–20 on the old Loos gauge), rake much as before, and little or no cap-shroud adjustment during the day. The lowers (D1) did the work through the wind range, so set the boat up to change them fast. The ram and backstay stayed constantly active. [W26]

## Crew and preparation
- Crew weight limit is 285 kg, with three or four aboard. Upwind, weight a little forward in light air and a little aft in breeze and waves. [E1]
- Develop Mojo's own tuning guide and keep a log of settings against feel and speed. Foil and mast alignment set the baseline for consistent speed. [E3, E5, E7]
- Arrive early and sail before the event; a training partner helps. Fresh jibs make a noticeable difference. [E9]
