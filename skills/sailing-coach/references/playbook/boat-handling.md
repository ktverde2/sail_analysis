# Boat handling

Tacks, gybes, sets and drops. Our maneuver table measures entry speed, minimum speed, `speed loss %`, recovery time and metres lost toward the mark (VMC). Speed loss and recovery explain a maneuver; metres lost toward the mark score it.

## Tacks
- In a heavy keelboat, turn slowly at first so the boat carries its momentum to windward, then faster once head-to-wind as speed drops. A long, slow tack in light air can gain real distance to windward. [B1, N1]
- In waves, turn faster: start on a wave face, bring the bow through as it pops over the crest, and let the next wave push the bow onto the new tack. [B1]
- Come out a few degrees low and squeeze up as speed builds; don't trim to full close-hauled straight away. Ease the jib lead forward or the jib tack for extra power on the exit; release the jib tack control to power up for acceleration. [B1, N2]
- Enter at or above target speed. Pick smooth water and a clear lane on the new tack; don't tack into another boat's bad air. [B1, B2]
- Crew: stay on the rail until the boat stands up; minimal movement; trimmer calls speed out of the tack so the helm knows when to squeeze. Hike immediately after the tack before any housekeeping. [B1, B2]
- Roll tacks in light and moderate air: heel to leeward to start the turn, roll the boat through head-to-wind, then flatten hard on the new side to accelerate. [B1, N1]
- **Data:** our July weekend ran 26–37% speed loss per tack, up to ~13 m per tack in the worst race. Compare races and wind ranges: where tacks are deeper (higher loss %, slower recovery), look at turn rate and how early the jib goes to full trim. Many tacks × metres per tack is the cost to set against shifts gained.
- **Cue:** "Slow in, fast through, low out, squeeze up."

## Gybes
- In breeze and waves, gybe the main first with the crew in the cockpit, then the pole once the boat is stable. [N1]
- Four-person crews gybe better than three: more weight to roll in light air, more hands to split the jobs in breeze. [N1]
- **Data:** gybe loss and recovery; gybes per run. More gybes than the shifts justify is a strategy question (see `downwind.md`).

## Sets and drops
- Set routine at the windward/offset mark: organized on the offset leg, lever eased, traveler down, kite sheet pre-set to its mark, bear away to the run angle before the kite fills. [N1]
- Drops: don't leave it late. A leeward drop is safer in a big breeze, ideally approaching on starboard. [N1, N3]
- Countdown the drop out loud (30 s warning, then 10 s) so the whole crew is in sync. [R4]

## Crew roles
- Clear roles reduce the noise: one trimmer owns the upwind mode, the kite trimmer calls height downwind, the tactician takes short-term control in tactical moments. [N4]
- In the start, the helm steers and the crew call time, distance and the go (see `starts.md`).
- In the chop at the 2026 Worlds, the best teams had at least two crew fully on speed, which freed the third (or fourth) from the trim loop to look up the course: tactics, the fleet, what's coming. When everyone is chasing a tenth of a knot, nobody is looking ahead. The lowers are the foredeck's job, so they have to feel the power the helm and trimmers feel. [W26]
- **Data:** side choices that ignored a persistent shift, or sailing into a header without tacking (`wind shifts and tack calls`), suggest nobody had their head out of the boat.
